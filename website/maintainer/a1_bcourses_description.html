Assignment 1 (due Monday, July 27 at 5:00pm)

A short solo warm-up, and the first step of a thread you carry through Assignment 2 and your final group project. Pick a county, set of counties, or region you care about. You will keep working with this same area all term.

The task

  1. Use get_acs() to pull one ACS variable that says something about housing precarity (rent burden, median rent, the share of households that rent…).
  2. Make one chart of it. A bar chart across your counties works well, and after Lab 3 you know to turn it sideways when the county names are long.
  3. Write 2–3 sentences describing what the chart shows and anything that surprised you.

Keep it simple. No bibliography or ASA formatting yet.

Step 1. Study the model answer, then close it

The class repo contains a complete example of this assignment, code/a1_example.R (in the Files pane on the DataHub, open the SOC-N100-Housing-Precarity-2026 folder, then code). Read it top to bottom and run it. It shows you the shape of a finished submission: a script that runs clean, a labeled chart saved with ggsave(), the interpretation sentences, and the AI-citation format.

Then close it and build your own. The example is a model, not a template. Your submission must be a fresh script you write yourself, about your place, with your variable and your sentences. Do not submit an edited copy of a1_example.R and do not reuse its text; your graders know the example line by line. Borrowing its structure (pull, chart, save, sentences) is exactly what it is for.

Step 2. Create and save a NEW R script (first time doing this, so here is the whole move)

  1. In RStudio on the DataHub: File > New File > R Script. A blank script opens. This one is yours.
  2. Write your assignment there. Start with library(tidyverse) and library(tidycensus) at the top, like every lab.
  3. File > Save. Name it like a1_lastname_firstname.R, and save it to your Home folder, the folder the save window usually starts in (if you are not sure, click Home in the save window’s sidebar).
  4. Important: do NOT save it inside the SOC-N100-Housing-Precarity-2026 class folder. That folder belongs to the course’s automatic updates. It refreshes from GitHub every time you click the class RStudio link, and files you add to it can be lost. Your Home folder is safe; the class folder is not yours to keep things in.
  5. For your chart, end your script with ggsave("~/a1_lastname_firstname.png", width = 8, height = 5) and run it. The ~/ means the image lands in that same safe Home folder.
  6. Before you submit, the one test that matters: Session > Restart R, then run your script top to bottom. If it runs clean on the DataHub, it will run for the grader.

Step 3. Download your two files from the DataHub to your computer

  1. In the Files pane (bottom right), click the house icon to open your Home folder. Your .R script and your .png chart are both there.
  2. Check the box next to your .R file, then click More (the gear icon) > Export and download it. Repeat for the .png.

Step 4. Upload to bCourses

  1. On this assignment page, click Start Assignment (or New Attempt if you are resubmitting).
  2. Under File Upload, click Choose File and select your .R script.
  3. Click + Add Another File and attach your chart image.
  4. Your 2–3 sentences can go in the Comments box when you submit, as comments inside your script, or in a third file (Word or PDF) with the chart pasted in. Any of those is fine.
  5. Click Submit Assignment. You should see a confirmation with a timestamp; that is your receipt.
